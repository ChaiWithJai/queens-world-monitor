# The Queens Print Room

Status: discussion brief
Reviewers: Imtiaz, Mo, Jai
Decision date: August 2, 2026
Public sale window under consideration: August 23–September 13, 2026

## Project overview

Open a temporary Queens gallery while the tennis world is in Flushing Meadows.
The work should feel culturally inevitable to MBIQ, not like unofficial
tournament merchandise. A six-print first volume gives the idea enough range to
feel curated while keeping proofing, rights, storefront, and fulfillment
manageable.

Working proposition: **The world comes to Queens. The gallery opens.**

## Problem

MBIQ has attention, Queens legitimacy, and visual archives, but no proven wall-art
category or owned launch list. The two risks are producing generic tennis work
and letting an apparently simple print drop turn into uncontrolled rights,
proofing, and fulfillment work.

## Audience

Primary:

- Queens natives and diaspora who buy identity, not souvenirs.
- Visitors already paying attention to Flushing Meadows during the tournament.
- MBIQ followers who engage with place, memory, and borough references.

Secondary:

- Collectors of accessible photography and graphic editions.
- Queens photographers and institutions whose work can widen the gallery.

## Goal and success criteria

The first volume should validate whether MBIQ can turn its cultural point of view
into a repeatable print format.

- A coherent six-print capsule with one unmistakable hero.
- Every production image has a signed license or is documented as owned/public
  domain before preorder.
- Two physical proof rounds maximum, approved before launch.
- One batch production handoff after the sale closes; no speculative inventory.
- Customer promise states edition, paper, dimensions, signing, and ship window
  before purchase.
- A reusable rights ledger, printer spec, product-page template, and launch
  checklist remain after the sale.

Revenue is a target, not the quality gate. Set the sales target only after paper,
packaging, artist share, payment fees, tax, replacements, and shipping are quoted.

## Creative principles

1. Queens is the subject; tennis is the moment.
2. Place beats event branding.
3. Designed for a wall first and an Instagram crop second.
4. Use real borough codes—court geometry, the 7, park infrastructure, languages,
   night light—without reducing Queens to icons.
5. Every treatment needs a reason to exist as print, not merely as apparel art.

## Product architecture

- **One hero:** owned MBIQ collage or newly commissioned borough piece; numbered
  edition of 100; target price $100 pending quote.
- **Three graphic editions:** open edition, time-limited; target price $55–$65.
- **Two artist editions:** licensed Queens photography or illustration; target
  price $150; artist terms decided before announcement.
- **Formats:** constrain to two sheet sizes and one paper family after proofs.
- **Mechanic:** preorder opens with Fan Week on August 23 and closes with the
  tournament on September 13; print once after close.

## Four treatment directions

### 01 — Court of Queens · recommended lead

Court lines become a borough grid: hard-court blue, ball-chartreuse, asphalt,
cream, condensed scoreboard typography, addresses and coordinates. This is the
most ownable bridge between the moment and MBIQ, and the safest route for owned
graphic editions.

### 02 — World of Tomorrow

Flushing Meadows as a century-long stage for the world: Unisphere, 1939 transit
maps, 1964 postal graphics, future-facing geometry. Warm ivory, oxidized red,
federal blue, silver. Strong archival depth; highest risk of familiar nostalgia.

### 03 — Seven Stops Out

The 7 line as narrative spine from the borough to the grounds. Transit purple,
safety yellow, stainless steel, overpass shadow, station numbering, multilingual
fragments. Best format for a sequential series or neighborhood edition.

### 04 — Night Session, Queens

Not stadium spectacle—the borough after dark. Flash photography, rain, electric
blue, acid green, black, small captions. Best for the two licensed artist
editions and the launch film; depends most heavily on finding the right
photographer.

## Scope and constraints

In scope:

- Six prints, edition logic, proofing, storefront, rights ledger, launch content,
  one post-close fulfillment batch.

Out of scope for Volume 1:

- Apparel, framing, custom sizes, international fulfillment promises before a
  quote, multiple printer workflows, paid events, or more than two partners.

Rights:

- Do not use US Open/USTA names as product marks, logos, event imagery, player
  likenesses, trophies, uniforms, ticket art, or trade dress without written
  permission. Factual internal references to the calendar are not a creative
  license. Final product names and campaign copy need a trademark/legal review.
- Archive availability is not permission. Record creator, owner, source URL,
  license, permitted use, territory, term, credit, and signed agreement for each
  image.
